--- VĂN BẢN ĐOẠN VĂN ---
At cutting-edge facilities, scientists are delving into how the gut's microbial inhabitants influence cancer progression. By throwing mice's microbial balance out of whack using antibiotics, researchers have noted distinct changes in immune responses and how tumors spread. Rather than viewing the disease in isolation, the medical community considers the microbiome a crucial indicator of a patient's lifestyle. The ultimate goal is to translate these discoveries into gentler therapies that keep metastasis at bay while respecting individual physiological differences.

This shift represents the core of personalized medicine, which factors in a person's genetic blueprint and daily habits. Historically, oncology relied on a rigid approach, subjecting patients to <u>grueling</u> regimens. However, contemporary clinics are turning the corner by classifying tumors based on specific genetic mutations. Doctors can now prescribe highly targeted interventions, explaining the sharp drop in mortality for certain cancers. <u>Rather than relying on blanket protocols, modern oncology tailors strategies to the unique biological signature of each patient.</u>

Beyond oncology, the burgeoning field of pharmacogenomics is making waves by matching prescriptions with a patient's inherited traits. A simple swab can bring to light genetic markers that warn of potential toxicity or indicate that a medication might fall flat. Crucially, <u>those</u> primarily dictate how the body processes drugs rather than predicting incurable diseases. While still in its infancy outside academic centers, this tailored approach is instrumental in dodging severe adverse reactions caused by minuscule dosing errors.

Despite these strides, the path forward is not entirely clear-cut. Recent regulatory approvals for genetic tests hint at a promising future, backed by massive data initiatives. Nevertheless, glaring disparities throw a spanner in the works. A disproportionate number of genomic studies lean heavily on individuals of European descent, severely <u>undermining</u> the universal applicability of the findings. True progress will hinge on expanding research diversity and ensuring that state-of-the-art diagnostic tools are accessible across all communities.

(Adapted from AAMC, “Making medicine personal: Moving away from a one-size-fits-all approach to health care,” Feb 22, 2024)

--- VĂN BẢN ĐOẠN VĂN ---
In the wake of recent economic upheaval, policymakers have begun re-evaluating the mechanisms used to underwrite decent livelihoods, particularly as traditional employment becomes increasingly precarious. [I] To prevent vulnerable demographics from falling through the cracks, advocates champion post-work guarantees – a synthesis of Participation Income and Universal Basic Services (UBS). Rather than dismantling the labor market, this paradigm seeks to establish an inviolable foundation, ensuring that no citizen endures conditions beneath a socially acceptable threshold.

At the heart of this framework lies the recalibration of financial assistance, steering away from the punitive conditionalities that plague conventional welfare. A universally distributed, tax-free stipend allows individuals to maintain a frugal yet dignified existence without the looming threat of the so-called benefit cliff. [II] When households can accept low-wage or intermittent work without abruptly losing state support, <u>they</u> are empowered to invest in skills rather than being trapped in a cycle of subsistence. Furthermore, Participation Income broadens the definition of productivity by adequately remunerating unpaid care and civic engagement, thereby validating contributions that traditional metrics often disregard.

Complementing direct cash transfers, UBS advocates for the collective, unconditional provision of absolute necessities, spanning from healthcare to public transit. Historically, market-first ideologies have engendered artificial scarcities, rendering essential participation prohibitively expensive for marginalized communities. [III] By instituting high-quality public infrastructure, governments can drastically <u>alleviate</u> the financial burdens placed on individuals, subsequently reducing the requisite size of any basic income scheme. Sturdy public services essentially function as an anchor for living standards, cushioning society against macroeconomic volatility.

Beyond mere fiscal mechanics, these guarantees serve profound civic and environmental objectives. Decoupling survival from coerced employment alleviates the relentless pressure to pursue perpetual economic growth at the expense of ecological stability. [IV] Consequently, individuals gain the autonomy to allocate their time across paid labor, volunteering, and restorative rest, fostering a more resilient and equitable societal fabric where human dignity is no longer contingent upon a wage slip.

(Adapted from Social Justice Ireland, “Basic Income and UBS key to improving living standards after COVID-19”)
